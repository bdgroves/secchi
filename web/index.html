secchi
a modern Secchi disk for Lake Tahoe

Watching clarity emerge from its drivers, in real time.

For over 150 years, limnologists have measured lake clarity by lowering a black-and-white disk into the water. secchi builds on that tradition with the Tahoe Environmental Observatory Network — around thirty-five sensors in the air, land, and water across the Lake Tahoe basin, streaming temperature, chlorophyll, dissolved oxygen, conductivity, and wave height in real time.

Same storm, two watersheds

TEON's sentinel sites — Blackwood Creek on the wet west side and Glenbrook Creek in the east-side rain shadow — are a deliberately contrasting pair. When a single atmospheric river or smoke plume hits the basin, both experience the same forcing but respond in wildly different ways.

Wildfire in the water

Smoke deposition delivers nutrients to the lake; nutrients feed algae; algae erode clarity. Overlaying NASA FIRMS active-fire detections and HRRR-Smoke plume forecasts on TEON's chlorophyll, dissolved-oxygen, and temperature signals lets us watch a fire's fingerprint arrive in the lake.
